Cognitive Biases in Recommender Systems

Tutorial at RecSys 2026, Minneapolis, United States — September 28, 2026

Abstract

Extensive research in psychology and sociology has revealed a plethora of cognitive biases that describe systematic deviations from rationality and objectivity in humans' cognitive processes and judgment, or collective prejudices of a society that favor one group's values, norms, and traditions over others. Examples such as confirmation bias, conformity bias, or cultural homophily are particularly relevant in the context of recommender systems. These biases are reflected in user-generated data, such as ratings or tags, and, as a consequence, are embedded in user models that drive recommendation algorithms. As a result, recommender systems may reproduce or amplify such biases, leading to undesired outcomes such as stereotypical recommendations, reduced diversity, or unfair exposure. At the same time, cognitive biases shape how users perceive, interact with, and provide feedback to recommender systems, thereby influencing feedback loops and long-term system dynamics. In this multidisciplinary tutorial, we bridge insights from psychology and computer science to examine cognitive biases that are particularly relevant in the context of recommender systems. We introduce these biases grounded in empirical findings and engage participants through interactive exercises that allow them to experience their effects firsthand. We then discuss how they manifest across the recommender system pipeline and outline strategies to mitigate negative effects, as well as opportunities to leverage biases for improved user engagement. Finally, we integrate a hands-on component in which participants will be able to: (1) design psychology-informed user choice models based on the presented cognitive biases; (2) integrate them into a provided feedback-loop simulation setup with established recommendation algorithms; and (3) observe how different biases influence the evolution of user profiles, recommendations, and system performance over time.
